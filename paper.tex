\begin{abstract}
We study full Gram computation for matrices whose successive rows have sparse
differences from a one-coordinate shift. A sequential factor-replacement schedule
evaluates a classical displacement-product identity while keeping every Gram
accumulator a sum of at most $n$ products of bounded old or new input factors.
For $A=2^eX$, integer $\abs{X_{ik}}\le B$, $nB^2\le2^{53}$ and
$-400\le e\le200$, this gives exact real-valued output in correctly rounded
binary64 arithmetic, independently of defect count and recurrence length.
The schedule uses two sparse support traversals without merging their supports.
Its Gram body uses $mn+2(P-m)+mQ$ fused multiply-add operations, where
$P=m(m+1)/2$ and $Q$ is the total shifted defect count. The implementation
validates the dense input and does not quantize it. Lean verifies the algebra,
prefix bounds and rounded reconstruction under an explicit primitive rounding
model; the machine interpretation and C execution remain separate trust
boundaries. We retain all 230 frozen comparisons with Accelerate SYRK and GEMM,
including losses. One full-output $768\times8192$ case takes $9.046$\,ms versus
SYRK's $19.634$\,ms, a paired speedup of $2.169$ with conditional within-run
95\% interval $[2.164,2.175]$. This is a scoped schedule, guarantee and software
contribution built from established algebra and bounded-integer arithmetic,
rather than a new unrestricted matrix-multiplication exponent or a priority claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and contribution}
Given $A\in\R^{m\times n}$, the target is $G=AA^T$, with either its lower
triangle or all $m^2$ entries materialized. In sliding data, neighboring rows may
share most shifted coordinates even when they are not exact windows of one
signal. Corrected samples and accumulated sparse innovations are two such
models. We exploit the shifted differences, not a low-rank hypothesis.

The central issue is arithmetic order. An exact polynomial identity does not
by itself guarantee that its floating-point intermediates are representable.
An order that expands both changes against the wrong factor can exceed the
capacity of a direct dot product, even when its final answer fits. We complete
all left-factor changes against the old right factors, then all right-factor
changes against the new left factors. Every prefix retains a bounded-factor
interpretation. This supplies an ordinary-dot sufficient capacity bound for
sparse reuse, with no dependence on recurrence length.

The contribution is the specified schedule, its complete invariant and
admission guarantee, and a validated implementation with retained experimental
evidence. The algebra and integer-in-floating-point principle are established.
We make no historical-first assertion, universal speedup claim, or claim that
dense-library wins establish structured state of the art. Section~\ref{sec:special}
adds a focused structured comparator and separates its new measurements from
the original campaign. No results from unrelated research projects are included.

\section{Relation to established results}\label{sec:related}
Pan, Murphy and Rosholt's compatible-matrix displacement product rule
\cite[Section 2.4, Theorem 2.3]{pan2008} has no low-rank prerequisite.
Let $L$ be the lower row shift, $C$ an orthogonal cyclic feature shift, and
$H=LAC$. Then the elementary product difference
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product}
 AA^T-HH^T=(A-H)H^T+A(A-H)^T
\end{equation}
is a specialization of that mechanism. The cyclic endpoint terms give the
removed head and inserted tail below. Sparse evaluation gives the work
mechanism; full residual rank does not make this algebra new.

FFLAS/FFPACK \cite[Sections 2.1.2 and 3.2.3]{fflas} uses bounded integer
accumulation on floating-point hardware and analyzes schedule-specific
intermediates. We inherit that representability principle. The local extra
content is the complete mixed-factor prefix argument for this sparse Gram
schedule, retaining $nB^2\le2^{53}$ without a defect-count term, together with
admission and software realization. This is a concrete elementary specialization.

STOMP \cite[Section IV-A, Equation (4)]{stomp} already gives the zero-defect
sliding-dot recurrence. Its matrix-profile output reduces pairwise results
rather than materializing a Gram matrix. SCAMP \cite[Sections 3.2.4 and 3.3]{scamp}
uses high-precision tile seeds and centered, rearranged updates for numerical
stability. Those scheduling and reseeding ideas are not new here. Gilda's
recent preprint \cite{gilda} analyzes periodically re-anchored sliding products
under different rounding assumptions; its formal-certification claims were not
independently audited for this release. We instead prove zero arithmetic error
for the explicit bounded dyadic class with arbitrary row-specific sparse changes.

These sources settle the inherited mechanisms, not universal priority of every
implementation detail. The artifact's contribution comparison states each
source's hypotheses and conclusions. No absence of a search hit is used as
evidence of historical priority. Nor do we compare complete STOMP/SCAMP systems
with a different output and validation contract.

\section{Construction and real-arithmetic proof}
For $i\ge1$ and $0\le k<n-1$, put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:defects}
 U_{ik}=A_{i-1,k+1},\qquad D_{ik}=A_{ik}-U_{ik},\qquad
 S_i=\supp D_i,\quad q_i=\abs{S_i},\quad Q=\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}q_i.
\end{equation}
Set $q_0=0$. No mathematical cap on $q_i$ is needed. Compute all first-column
anchors $G_{i0}=\sum_{k<n}A_{ik}A_{0k}$. For increasing $i=1,\ldots,m-1$ and
$j=1,\ldots,i$, use the following algorithm. Each displayed endpoint or
correction operation is one explicit FMA in the C implementation.

\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{.91\linewidth}
\small
\begin{verbatim}
Validate A; extract ordered nonzero residual lists S_i.
During the same feature traversal, compute first-column anchors.
for i = 1,...,m-1:
    for j = 1,...,i:
        v = G[i-1,j-1]
        v = fma(-A[i-1,0], A[j-1,0], v)
        v = fma( A[i,n-1], A[j,n-1], v)
        for k in S_i: v = fma(D[i,k], U[j,k], v)
        for k in S_j: v = fma(A[i,k], D[j,k], v)
        G[i,j] = v
Reflect the strict lower triangle if full output is requested.
\end{verbatim}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}
The C anchors use eight independent chains followed by a tree over disjoint
coordinate subsets. The first correction loop must finish before the second;
order within either distinct-coordinate list is arbitrary. Supports can overlap.

\begin{lemma}[Complete prefix invariants]\label{lem:prefix}
Fix $1\le j\le i<m$ and an exact predecessor $G_{i-1,j-1}$. After removing
the head product and inserting the tail, any prefix $P\subseteq S_i$ of the
first correction loop has accumulator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:first}
 V_1(P)=A_{i,n-1}A_{j,n-1}+\sum_{k<n-1}L_P(k)U_{jk},\qquad
 L_P(k)=\begin{cases}A_{ik}&k\in P,\\U_{ik}&k\notin P.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
After that loop is complete, any prefix $T\subseteq S_j$ of the second has
\begin{equation}\label{eq:second}
 V_2(T)=A_{i,n-1}A_{j,n-1}+\sum_{k<n-1}A_{ik}R_T(k),\qquad
 R_T(k)=\begin{cases}A_{jk}&k\in T,\\U_{jk}&k\notin T.\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The predecessor equals its head product plus
$\sum_{k<n-1}U_{ik}U_{jk}$. The two endpoint operations therefore give
\eqref{eq:first} at the empty prefix. Inserting a new support coordinate $k$
adds $(A_{ik}-U_{ik})U_{jk}$, replacing exactly its left factor; induction
proves every first prefix. Outside $S_i$ the old and new factors coincide,
so the completed first loop equals \eqref{eq:second} at $T=\varnothing$.
Adding $A_{ik}(A_{jk}-U_{jk})$ replaces exactly the next right factor.
This proves every second prefix, including overlapping supports and $i=j$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Real recurrence and reconstruction]\label{thm:real}
For arbitrary real $A$ and $n\ge1$, the ordered update is
\begin{align}\label{eq:recurrence}
G_{ij}={}&G_{i-1,j-1}-A_{i-1,0}A_{j-1,0}+A_{i,n-1}A_{j,n-1}\notag\\
 &+\sum_{k\in S_i}D_{ik}U_{jk}+\sum_{k\in S_j}A_{ik}D_{jk}.
\end{align}
Exact first-column anchors and these updates reconstruct the entire lower
triangle of $AA^T$ for every finite $m$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
The final value in \eqref{eq:second} is the desired row dot product, proving
\eqref{eq:recurrence}. Its predecessor belongs to an earlier row and is itself
lower triangular. Row induction, with the first-column anchors as base cases,
proves all required entries. There is no output for $m=0$. For $n=0$ define
the result as zero separately. At $n=1$ the support sums are empty; deletion
cancels the predecessor's sole product and insertion gives the new product.
\end{proof}
Only distributivity and ordered factor replacement are used: no scalar factors
are commuted in the local identity. With appropriate adjoints this also gives
an ordered block identity, but no recursive block implementation or speed claim
is inferred from it.

\section{Exact binary64 execution}
\begin{definition}[Mathematical admission class]\label{def:class}
For $n\ge1$, let $X\in\Z^{m\times n}$, $B\in\Z_{\ge0}$ and $e\in\Z$ satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:admission}
 A_{ik}=2^eX_{ik},\quad \abs{X_{ik}}\le B,\quad nB^2\le2^{53},
 \qquad -400\le e\le200.
\end{equation}
Arithmetic is IEEE binary64 with correctly rounded addition, subtraction,
multiplication and explicit FMA, no reassociation changing the schedule, and
no flushing of input values during admission. FMA rounds $ab+c$ once.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Intermediate coefficient bound]\label{lem:bound}
In coefficient units $2^{2e}$, every anchor-chain state and reduction node,
the deleted-head state, and every value in \eqref{eq:first}--\eqref{eq:second}
is an integer of magnitude at most $nB^2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Each state is a sum of at most $n$ products of actual old or new input
coefficients. Each factor has magnitude at most $B$, so the triangle
inequality bounds the sum by $nB^2$. Anchor chains select disjoint coordinates;
tree nodes join disjoint subsets, preserving the same property. Deletion
leaves $n-1$ products. Insertion and the two prefix identities give $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact arithmetic]\label{thm:fp}
Under Definition~\ref{def:class}, residual subtractions, every Gram anchor
operation, both endpoint FMAs and every correction FMA are exact as real
operations, normal when nonzero, and finite. The lower output equals $AA^T$
exactly, independently of $m$, defect count and recurrence length.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Every integer of magnitude at most $2^{53}$ is exactly representable in
binary64. An accumulator is $z2^{2e}$ with integer $\abs z\le2^{53}$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:bound}. Its nonzero magnitude is at least $2^{-800}$ and its
magnitude is at most $2^{453}$. Its significant-bit length fits the format,
and the exponent range is safely normal and finite. Thus its exact real
value is representable; correct rounding returns it unchanged. Induction over
each chain, tree node and update, and then over predecessor rows, proves exactness.

For residuals, $n\ge1$ implies $B<2^{27}$, so each difference has integer
coefficient of magnitude at most $2B<2^{28}$ at scale $2^e$. It is representable,
normal when nonzero and finite. If a residual product exists then $n\ge2$ and
its coefficient is at most $2B^2\le2^{53}$; at $n=1$ none occurs. Hence the
subtractions and their operands are exact as well. No premise assumes an
exact intermediate or an exact anchor: those properties were derived.
\end{proof}
This proves zero real error, including cancellation zeros and nearly dependent
rows. It is stronger than the campaign's fixed requirement
$\abs{\widehat G_{ij}-G_{ij}}\le2\cdot10^{-12}\|A_i\|_2\|A_j\|_2$.
Signed-zero bit patterns are not identified with a unique real encoding.
The square-root estimate used to select an admission cap is not asserted to
be an exact real square root; integer comparisons correct its result.

\subsection{A strict grouped-order counterexample}\label{sec:witness}
The alternative grouped corrections $D_iA_j^T+U_iD_j^T$ have the same final
polynomial but do not inherit the ordinary-dot bound automatically. Take
$n=2$, $e=0$, $B=2^{26}$ and rows $[0,-B]$, $[B-1,B]$. There is one defect,
and $nB^2=2^{53}$. Both orders reach $2B^2$ after the endpoint operations.
The grouped first correction requests
\[
2B^2+(2B-1)(B-1)=4B^2-3B+1=18014398308155393,
\]
which round-to-nearest ties-to-even changes to $18014398308155392$.
Its final answer is $9007199120523264$, whereas the exact, representable
diagonal is $(B-1)^2+B^2=9007199120523265$. The sequential order first computes
$2B^2+(2B-1)(-B)=B=67108864$, then returns the exact diagonal. The independent
verifier executes these FMAs. This is a strict comparison with that local
grouped order, not a lower bound against every published algorithm.

\section{Implementation contract and cost}
The public routine writes the lower triangle of a row-major, strided double
matrix. Inputs and outputs must be disjoint valid allocated objects. Its
implementation restrictions, separate from the mathematical theorem, are
$n\le65536$, \textnormal{\texttt{max\_q}}$\le64$, and $-400\le e\le200$.
For nonempty input it uses
\[
 B_n=\left\lfloor\sqrt{\left\lfloor2^{53}/n\right\rfloor}\right\rfloor.
\]
The quotient and final square tests use integer arithmetic; a floating square
root provides only an initial estimate. For every actual input it checks
finiteness, the cap, $x=A_{ik}2^{-e}=\operatorname{trunc}(x)$ and
$x2^e=A_{ik}$. The last check rejects tiny invalid values that scale to zero.
No projection or quantization is performed. The shifted support cap is checked
before every residual store. Validation, exact residual extraction and eight
anchor chains share a feature traversal; NEON combines predicates over eight
values before branching. A scalar tail of one through eight values prevents
shifted loads beyond the row.

Status zero certifies exact output. Negative codes report invalid pointers,
strides or extents ($-1$), nonfinite input or excess defects ($-2$), allocation
failure ($-3$), or unsupported lattice/parameter class ($-4$). Error-code
precedence for multiply-invalid inputs can differ between scalar and vector
paths; both reject. Discard partial output on error. Extents and allocation
products are checked before use. At $m=0$ the routine returns without accesses.
At $m>0,n=0$ it validates output extents and parameter caps, then writes lower
zeros without reading $A$. At $n=1$ residual storage is absent. There is no
automatic fallback to a different numerical domain.

\begin{proposition}[Gram work and requested workspace]\label{prop:work}
For $m,n>0$, put $P=m(m+1)/2$ and $D=P-m$. The Gram body uses exactly
\begin{equation}\label{eq:work}
 F=mn+2D+mQ
\end{equation}
FMAs. With $c=\min(\textnormal{\texttt{max\_q}},n-1)$, its requested heap workspace on the
recorded 64-bit ABI is $16(m-1)c+8(m+1)$ bytes if $(m-1)c>0$, and zero otherwise.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Anchors use $mn$ FMAs, and the $D$ recurrence entries use two endpoint FMAs
each. Residual row $t\ge1$ occurs $t$ times in the first correction role and
$m-t$ times in the second, thus exactly $m$ times. This proves \eqref{eq:work},
including both correction occurrences on diagonal entries. The implementation
reserves $(m-1)c$ records of one eight-byte index and one eight-byte residual,
and $m+1$ eight-byte offsets only when capacity is positive. No norm/error
array, packed dense input, second Gram buffer or repair dot is allocated.
\end{proof}
Admission separately performs $2mn$ scaling multiplications and predicates;
source-level anchor reductions add $7m$ additions, and extraction uses $Q$
subtractions. Thus \eqref{eq:work} is not a complete instruction count.
At $n=1$ this schedule uses $m^2$ FMAs, and need not be efficient. At $n=0$
it performs no FMAs; the nonempty work formula is not applied to that branch.

The caller still stores $8mn$ input bytes and $8m^2$ output bytes. At
$768\times8192$, cap two, requested scratch is $30{,}696$ bytes versus a
48\,MiB input and 4.5\,MiB output. With two defects in every later row,
the Gram body has $8{,}058{,}624$ FMAs, compared with $2{,}419{,}064{,}832$
classical SYRK product terms, but admission adds $12{,}582{,}912$ multiplications.

A reuse-aware preparation operand model is $8[2mn+(m-1)(n-1)]$ bytes, plus
up to 128 source-operand bytes per changed eight-value block if scalar
extraction rereads all its lanes. Recurrence output stores and predecessor
reads together demand $8m^2$ logical bytes; endpoints, correction factors
and residual records add $32D$, $8mQ$ and $16mQ$ bytes before reuse. Offset
accesses and control add costs. Full reflection adds $16D$ logical bytes.
These are transparent source-access models, not measured DRAM traffic or
retired-load counts. Cached rows and register hoisting can remove physical
transfers. Vendor internal workspace and total process peak memory were not
instrumented. Small-case losses below demonstrate that product counts alone
do not predict wall-clock speed.

The original 230 runs and every individual sample are retained unchanged.
The candidate was fixed as method 13 before reserved evaluation. Each run
compares Accelerate DSYRK, native full DGEMM, an earlier general-real certified

\subsection{Focused structured comparator}\label{sec:special}
For a same-contract alternative based on known ingredients, reconstruct a
Hankel generator $h$ from the actual first row and later row tails:
$h_k=A_{0k}$ for $k<n$ and $h_{n-1+i}=A_{i,n-1}$ for $i\ge1$. Set
$H_{ik}=h_{i+k}$ and $E=A-H$. A standalone comparator computes $HH^T$
using the classical sliding recurrence, then applies
\[
 AA^T=HH^T+EH^T+AE^T
\]
with all left replacements preceding the right replacements. Its factors
come from actual input entries, so the same bounded-factor proof gives
exactness. It validates the lattice and local shifted cap, counts and allocates
actual $E$, and includes generator reconstruction, all scans, corrections,
allocation and full reflection in the timed call. It receives no latent
generator or trusted edit locations. This is an implementation derived here
from established ingredients, not an asserted published implementation.

Its correction count depends on $R=\operatorname{nnz}(E)$ rather than $Q$:
$mn+2D+(m+1)R$ Gram FMAs (corrections include the first column). Corrected windows may have small $R$, whereas accumulated
innovations can make $R$ large even when $Q$ is small. The comparison is useful
precisely because it exposes that structural distinction. It is a focused
baseline, not a comprehensive structured-library contest.

The new 20-run campaign is stored separately from the original 230. Before
timing it freezes $512\times4096$, cap two, $e=-15$, full output, both families,
uniform/nearly constant coefficients and the same five seeds. Four methods
(SYRK, GEMM, sequential, reconstructed-Hankel) receive 32 paired samples in
eight balanced blocks of four, with five warmups and 10\,ms calibration.
Independent generator fingerprints verify seed and input identity; a
source/binary-bound manifest detects missing, duplicated or inconsistent jobs.
No original measurement is replaced.

The formal arithmetic model makes the machine boundary explicit. Define
\[
 \mathcal D(s)=\{zs:z\in\Z,\ \abs z\le2^{53}\}.
\]
A \texttt{RoundingModel} supplies a function $r:\R\to\R$ that fixes
$\mathcal D(s)$. Rounded FMA is $r(ab+c)$. Integer prefix bounds and ring
identities derive primitive and algorithmic exactness; no Gram conclusion,
exact anchor or exact prefix is postulated. Constructed rounded anchors
discharge the intermediate reconstruction theorem's anchor premise.

\texttt{Binary64Bridge} assumes only that the interpreted rounding fixes
$\mathcal D(2^a)$ for $-1022\le a\le970$. This subset is analytically
representable in binary64: coefficients have at most 53 significant bits
(the endpoint $2^{53}$ is itself a power of two), minimum nonzero exponent
$-1022$, and maximum leading exponent $970+53=1023$.
Lean proves the input and doubled exponent containment and transports the
abstract rounded reconstruction to the real Gram target. It does not prove
a bit-level IEEE encoding theorem or instantiate a hardware rounding function.
This primitive correspondence remains an exposed hypothesis, not an axiom
asserting the desired algorithm conclusion.

The final theorem admits arbitrary natural feature count, including zero,
unbounded row indices restricted by $j\le i$, integer coefficients and the
admission bound. A finite matrix is represented by zero extension. Lean uses
$N=0$ or $N=n+1$ to handle the tail coordinate. Its folds accept every
distinct support enumeration; a \texttt{foldr} is matched to the increasing
C scan by reversing its supplied list. The canonical formal anchors are a
direct chain, and a separate disjoint-merge lemma supports the C eight-chain
tree. Residual subtraction exactness is a separate proved bridge to the
scaled coefficient differences used by the formal update.

These are mathematical correspondence arguments. Lean does not verify the C
program, NEON loads, allocation safety, compiler, signed-zero representation,
timing harness or wall-clock measurements. Source audits, strict tests and
retained receipts provide separate evidence for those delivered artifacts.
The trust base includes the Lean kernel/distribution and pinned imported
libraries; cached dependencies were used rather than rebuilding all mathlib.

provenance and checksum manifest. Recorded evidence is usable without rerunning
the performance campaign. The version tag and publication receipts identify

exactness guarantee with a scoped implementation result, while preserving
the limitations and losing cases needed to interpret that result.
